&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Since I spent a lot of time last week on the kinesthetic sculpture, I decided to do something more straightforward this week. I chose to improve my kinesthetic sculpture and adapt it to use the potentiometer we learned how to use in the lab. The first thing I did was convert all the gears into cardboard, create an opening in the moving gear where I could embed the magnet, and build a box to use as a structure.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; To improve on my strategy from last week, which was just sticking the motor in without any support, I laser-cut some cardboard to hold it up. My initial plan was to screw the motor into the cardboard, but it worked well enough without needing screws. The wiring was a bit trickier, and at one point I burned my fingers a little. Luckily, Bobby helped me figure out what was wrong, and eventually, I got everything working together.
